# Diplomacy and Subjects

## Alliances and marriages

Alliances commit both parties to join each other's wars (subject to AI willingness, see "attitude" —
an ally with a negative attitude toward you may still refuse). Royal marriages are a softer diplomatic
tool (raise relations, historically enable personal unions) with no war obligation. Both are the main
lever a small country has to survive against a bigger one — "find allies before you need them" is the
recurring advice across guides for weaker nations.

## Subject types compared

| Subject | Overlord gets | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vassal** | Partial tax, force limit boost | Cheapest, most common; auto-joins overlord's wars; integratable after 10 years |
| **Personal Union (PU)** | Military access, can inherit outright | Two crowns under one ruler; junior partner keeps own institutions until union ends/is annexed |
| **Tributary** | Tribute income, little control | Highest autonomy for the subject, least overlord control |
| **March** | Military-focused, pays no tax | Subject at the frontier, boosts overlord's force limit more than a vassal |
| **Colonial nation** | Tariff income | Automatically formed from overseas colonial provinces past a development threshold |

All non-trade-company subjects have a **liberty desire** stat driving how likely they are to seek
independence — the practical question for "should I annex this subject" is almost always about
liberty desire and integration cost, not the subject type's flavor text.

## The Holy Roman Empire (HRE)

A unique political structure covering the Germanic/northern-Italian region: member "Princes" vote via
up to 7 Prince-**Electors** for the **Emperor**, who gains authority and pushes centralizing/
decentralizing reforms. For a nation physically inside or bordering the HRE, its state (religious peace,
current Emperor, reform progress) is often more decision-relevant than any single neighbor's army size —
taking HRE land as a non-Emperor, non-elector is generally destabilizing unless you (and your allies)
can absorb the diplomatic fallout. For a nation outside Europe entirely, the HRE is close to irrelevant
and can usually be ignored in an answer.

## Prestige and Power Projection

**Prestige** reflects how favorably other nations view your nation's standing; among other effects, it
reduces the AE impact of your actions (see `fundamentals:war_and_expansion`). **Power projection** rises
from recent notable military/diplomatic actions and, at 50+, adds +1/month to all three monarch power
types (`fundamentals:growth_engine`) — both are long-term diplomatic assets worth managing even outside
of active wars.
