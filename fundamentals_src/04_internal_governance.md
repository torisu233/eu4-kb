# Internal Governance

## Three independent progress axes

Easy to conflate, but these are three separate tracks: **Government type** (Monarchy/Republic/
Theocracy/Tribal — the broad category with its own mechanics), **Government rank** (Duchy → Kingdom →
Empire — a prestige/scale tier reached via development+prestige thresholds, or granted instantly by many
formation decisions), and **Government reform tier** (the sequence of reforms unlocked by accumulating
reform progress within your government type). Rank bonuses **replace, not stack** (Empire's bonus alone,
not Duchy's + Kingdom's + Empire's), and rank never downgrades on its own — the main exception is that
subjects (vassals, colonial nations, etc.) are always capped at Duchy rank and lose any higher rank when
subjugated. Idea group slots are **not** gated by rank — don't assume rank governs idea-slot access.
Rank affects national focus cooldown (`fundamentals:growth_engine`), free leader upkeep, governing
capacity, and absolutism cap, so "how do I get more of X" questions often have "raise rank" as part of
the answer.

## Estates

**Estates** (nobility, clergy, burghers, and government-type/region-specific ones like cossacks or
brahmins) hold provincial influence in exchange for privileges — granting a privilege typically gives a
national bonus (sometimes including flat monarch power/month) at the cost of estate loyalty and land
share. Low estate loyalty risks a rebellious faction; this is the main tool for extracting extra bonuses
without spending monarch power directly, at the cost of some centralized control.

## Stability and Corruption

**Stability** (roughly -3 to +3) affects almost every internal rate — unrest, tech cost, trade,
autonomy spread — and is raised by spending ADM. **Corruption** rises passively (faster the further
your capital is from your provinces, or in large empires) and depletes income/efficiency until reduced
via a national focus, decision, or specific buildings/ideas — treat sustained high corruption as a slow
economic bleed, not a one-off event.

## Religion and Culture

Provinces with an unaccepted culture (especially outside your culture group, a bigger penalty) generate
extra unrest and produce less; converting them to an accepted culture or granting cultural acceptance
via ideas/policies is the standard fix. Religious conversion requires the province's religion to first
match your ability to convert it (via missionary strength) and is blocked in some cases by very low
tolerance gaps. Both culture and religion homogeneity reduce unrest and raise the ceiling on how
effectively a large multi-ethnic empire can be run.
