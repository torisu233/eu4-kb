# Economy and Colonization

## Where income comes from

Three base income sources per province: **tax** (from base tax development), **production** (from base
production development and the province's trade good, sold at market price), and **trade** (collected
via trade power in trade nodes, see below). Buildings and local autonomy modify all three; high local
autonomy is one of the most common reasons a large empire still feels poor.

## Trade nodes

The map's 80 trade nodes each pool the trade value of their member provinces; trade flows from
"upstream" nodes into "downstream" ones, ending at one of the three end nodes (Genoa, Venice, English
Channel). Your **home node** (usually where your capital/trade capital sits) is where you collect trade
income without a distance penalty. Controlling provinces with trade-power modifiers (estuaries, Centers
of Trade) inside a node, sending merchants to **steer** trade toward your home node, and sending light
ships to **protect trade** in coastal nodes are the three main levers to increase your take. If your
capital isn't in an end node, prioritize a node with few outgoing paths — fewer competitors fight over
that trade.

## Budget maintenance sliders

Three sliders control ongoing spend: army maintenance, navy maintenance, and missionary/colonist
maintenance. Missionary/colonist sliders should generally stay maxed (conversion/colonization speed
scales with them, and they cost nothing when idle). Navy maintenance can safely drop to 50-75% at peace
(cheaper, but a lower-morale fleet caught out of port is more vulnerable). Army maintenance is the most
situational: at war it should be 100%, but at peace it can often drop to 50-75% for a real economic
boost — as long as the under-maintained regiments aren't stationed near a border or in unrest, since low
morale/no reinforcement makes them a liability if attacked.

## Colonization

Sending colonists requires the **Exploration** or **Expansion** idea group (Exploration is generally
better for finding new land yourself via Explorers; Expansion suits nations that already border
uncolonized land). Taking both idea groups gives up to 3 simultaneous colonists. **Conquistadors** and
**Explorers** function like land/naval leaders but also reveal terra incognita. Overseas colonial
provinces produce tariff income instead of full tax, but often carry valuable trade goods.
