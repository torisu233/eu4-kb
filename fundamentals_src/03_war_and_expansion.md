# War and Expansion

## Manpower and Force Limit

**Manpower** is the pool of men available to reinforce and rebuild regiments; it regenerates over time
and is drained by casualties and attrition. **Force limit** is a soft cap on army/navy size — units
above it aren't blocked, but cost sharply more upkeep, so it's the practical ceiling on a sustainable
army.

## Casus Belli, warscore, and peace

Declaring war without a valid **Casus Belli (CB)** carries a stability hit and aggressive expansion
penalty (see below). CBs come from missions, claims, provoked triggers, or religious/imperial CBs.
During a war, **warscore** (built from battles won, sieges, occupation) determines how much you can
demand in the peace deal — occupying provinces you actually want before offering peace matters more
than winning battles in open field.

## War Exhaustion

Accumulates on both sides based on casualties and war duration; high war exhaustion causes unrest,
attrition, and rising desire for peace on both sides. In a war against a much larger opponent, driving
up *their* war exhaustion (by prolonging the war, avoiding decisive losses) is often the only realistic
path to an acceptable peace.

## Aggressive Expansion → Coalitions (the most common beginner trap)

Taking territory or aggressive peace terms generates **Aggressive Expansion (AE)** against nations that
care about the target (by religion, culture, distance, or relation to the loser) — it is a negative
relations modifier, decaying by 2/year by default. If a nation's AE-driven opinion of you drops enough,
its attitude turns **Outraged**, and once **at least 4** such nations (each with ≥-50 AE-relations modifer
and negative opinion of you) exist without a truce with you, they can form a **Coalition** and jointly
declare war — usually a much worse fight than any single war would have been. Coalitions are unlikely to
form if the combined strength of potential members is clearly weaker than you and your allies. Practical
implication: expanding fast is fine, expanding **without pauses to let AE decay, or without enough
military/alliance strength to shrug off a coalition**, is what actually gets punished — this is the
mechanic behind most "why did everyone suddenly declare war on me" situations.

## Core vs Claim

Both reduce the cost/consequence of taking a province, but are not the same thing: a **Claim** is a
temporary, cheaper justification to *take* a specific province in a war (reduces AE and warscore cost
for it) without needing a full core first. A **Core** is the permanent, low-upkeep ownership status that
reduces a province's ongoing unrest/autonomy cost once you already hold it. Claim lowers the cost of
acquiring; core lowers the cost of keeping.
